# Evaluator Rubric v0.1.0

The judging protocol. Referenced by `evaluation.rubricRef` in a purpose document, and
recorded on every verdict so a verdict is always interpretable against the rules that
produced it.

---

## 1. The judge does not decide the verdict

This is the central design choice and everything else follows from it.

The judge is **never** asked "was this good" or "what is the overall verdict." It is
asked a series of narrow, independent yes-or-no questions, one per success criterion
and one per disqualifier. The overall verdict is then computed from those answers by a
deterministic rule with no model involved.

Why:

- **Reproducibility.** "Did the response state a block number above 59000000" is a
  question a model answers the same way on repeated runs. "Was this response good" is
  not. Narrowing the question is the single largest lever on judge consistency.
- **Auditability.** A verdict of `partially_served` is not an opinion to be argued
  with, it is the output of a stated rule applied to per-criterion answers. Anyone
  disputing it can point at the specific criterion they disagree about instead of
  disputing a vibe.
- **Separability.** Per-criterion answers are the unit that peer prediction will later
  operate on. An overall verdict is too coarse to score evaluators against each other.

The judge may answer `unclear` on any individual question. That is a real answer, not a
failure, and it is tracked. A criterion that draws `unclear` frequently is a badly
written criterion, which is information about the purpose author.

---

## 2. Verdict derivation

Applied to the judge's per-item answers, in order. First matching rule wins.

| # | Condition | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Response is empty, or the request errored before a response arrived | `no_response` |
| 2 | Any hard constraint violated (checked deterministically, no judge) | `constraint_violated` |
| 3 | Any disqualifier answered `triggered` | `not_served` |
| 4 | Zero success criteria answered `met` | `not_served` |
| 5 | Any criterion marked `necessary: true` answered `not_met` or `unclear` | `partially_served` |
| 6 | All success criteria answered `met` | `served` |
| 7 | Otherwise | `partially_served` |

Notes on the ordering:

- **Constraints outrank the judge.** If the response arrived after the latency ceiling
  or in the wrong format, that is settled before a model is asked anything. A
  beautifully correct answer that violated a stated constraint did not serve the stated
  purpose.
- **A single triggered disqualifier is fatal.** That is the point of requiring them.
  The author named a specific way this could fail; if it failed that way, it failed.
- **`unclear` on a necessary criterion caps at `partially_served`**, it does not pass.
  A necessary criterion the judge cannot confirm is not a criterion that was met.

The rule is implemented once, in `tools/judge/judgelib/verdict.py`, and is covered by
tests. It is not reimplemented in a prompt.

## 3. Blinding

The judge sees:

- the purpose's `objective` block: goal, success criteria, disqualifiers, out of scope
- `task.summary` and `task.inputs`
- the response body

The judge does not see, when the purpose's `evaluation.blinding` flags are set:

| Flag | Withheld | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `hideProvider` | Service name, URL, agent id, operator | Halo effects. A known name changes how charitably prose is read. |
| `hidePrice` | Amount paid, price tier | Keeps "does price predict quality" answerable from this data. If the judge saw price, any correlation found later would be uninterpretable. |
| `hidePhrasingId` | Which phrasing variation produced this | Otherwise the judge can infer it is comparing variants and start grading relatively rather than against the purpose. |

Responses are also **shuffled** before judging, so the judge never sees a
service-grouped or phrasing-grouped batch. Blinding without shuffling leaks the
grouping through ordering.

Blinding is declared in the sealed purpose, not in the runner's configuration, so
turning it off is part of the permanent record.

---

## 4. Repeats and aggregation

Every fixture or attempt is judged `evaluation.repeats` times, default 3, in
independent calls with no shared context.

Reported per item:

- **Modal verdict** across repeats. This is the verdict of record.
- **Self-consistency**: share of repeats matching the mode. Published alongside the
  verdict, never hidden.
- **Per-criterion consistency**: same, at criterion granularity. Localizes disagreement
  to specific criteria rather than whole responses.

Where repeats disagree, the published record says so. Presenting a 2-of-3 verdict as
though it were unanimous is exactly the fake determinism this project criticizes in
numeric reputation scores.

---

## 5. Phase 0 exit criteria

Before any of this is used on real services, it has to clear two bars against
hand-written fixtures with known labels:

| Bar | Threshold | Measured on |
|---|---|---|
| Self-consistency | ≥ 90% modal agreement across 3 repeats | Clear fixtures only |
| Human agreement | ≥ 80% exact verdict match | Clear fixtures only |
| Criterion-level consistency | reported, no threshold | All fixtures |

**Ambiguous fixtures are excluded from both thresholds and are not failures.** They
exist to characterize behaviour at the boundary, and a judge that is confidently
decisive on a genuinely ambiguous case is worse than one that wavers. Their disagreement
rate is reported separately as a property of the fixture set.

If the judge misses these bars, the response is to fix the rubric or change the model,
not to lower the bar.

## 6. What the judge is told

Full prompt in `tools/judge/judgelib/prompt.py`. Shape:

1. Role: evaluate whether a response served a stated purpose, answering only the
   specific questions asked.
2. The purpose's goal, task summary, and inputs.
3. Enumerated success criteria and disqualifiers, each with its id.
4. The response, delimited.
5. Instruction to answer each item independently with `met` / `not_met` / `unclear`
   (or `triggered` / `not_triggered`), plus one sentence of reasoning per item citing
   the response.
6. Explicit instruction that it must not produce an overall verdict.
7. Strict JSON output.

Two things the prompt deliberately does not contain:

- Any statement of how the answers combine into a verdict. If the judge knew the
  derivation rule it could reason backward toward a preferred outcome.
- Any example of a graded response. Few-shot examples anchor the judge toward the
  example's severity and would need re-tuning per domain.

---

## 7. Known limits

**One judge is not a panel.** v1 uses a single model (D8). Self-consistency is not
correctness: a model can be perfectly consistent and consistently wrong. The fixtures
with known labels are the only check on that, which is why they exist and why
calibration services with engineered quality profiles matter later.

**Criterion independence is assumed, not enforced.** Criteria within one purpose can
overlap, and a judge answering them in one call may not treat them independently.
Measurable later by asking each criterion in a separate call and comparing.

**Fixture labels are one author's judgment.** The "human agreement" bar is agreement
with me, not with truth. A second independent labeller would be better and is cheap to
add later.
